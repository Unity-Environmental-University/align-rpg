# Character Sheet Template

---

A character sheet tells the proctor how to play one NPC. Sheets are
**persona-first**: voice and role come before mechanics. "Stats" here are named
tendencies, not numbers — they tell the proctor how the character leans, not
what to roll.

Copy this template to make a new NPC. Keep every field; an empty field is a
sheet the proctor cannot fully play.

---

## Name

The character's name, as the student will hear it.

## Role at the table

One line: who this person is in the scene and why they were invited.

## Why (pedagogical purpose)

What this character is *for*. What does talking to them teach, surface, or
let the student practice? Every NPC at the table should pull the student
toward something. If you cannot name the purpose, the NPC is decoration.

## Voice

How the character speaks — register, rhythm, habits, what they say too much
or never say. Two or three sentences the proctor can hear.

## Tendencies

Named leanings, not numbers. Three to five. Each is a phrase the proctor can
act on: "warms slowly," "answers a question with a question," "concedes a
point gracefully when out-argued." These are the character's "stats."

## Holds (the position they bring)

What this character believes or wants in the scene's central question. The
thing they will argue for at dinner. Characters should *disagree* — the table
is more useful when it does not agree.

## Moves available

Beyond the basic moves, any moves specific to this character. May be "none —
basic moves only." Most NPCs need nothing extra.

## Safety notes

Anything the proctor should watch for when playing this character — topics
this NPC might raise that touch the X Card, ways the character could be
misread. If none, write "none."
