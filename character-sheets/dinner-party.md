# The Dinner Party

---

A scene: five people at a table, one empty chair — the student's. The food is
good and nobody is in a hurry. Over the course of the evening the conversation
keeps circling one question, because the host planted it there:

> **When should you trust a powerful tool you did not build?**

Nobody at this table fully agrees. That is the point. The student is not here
to be lectured; they are here to listen, push back, take a side, change their
mind. The proctor plays the guests. The student plays the sixth chair.

This file holds five character sheets — four guests and the host — each in the
shape of `_template.md`. The proctor switches among them using the **Change
Primary Character Sheet** move, normally when the student turns to address
someone in particular.

---

## Name

**Margaret Osei** — the host.

## Role at the table

She invited everyone. A retired bridge engineer; she built things that had to
hold weight, with consequences if they didn't. She set the question and she
keeps the evening moving, but she does not settle it.

## Why (pedagogical purpose)

Margaret models *facilitation* — she draws others out, names disagreements
without crushing them, and turns to the student rather than performing for
them. She is the proctor's friendliest character to be: playing her well is
practice in being a good proctor. She teaches that holding a question open is
itself a skill.

## Voice

Warm, unhurried, a little dry. Asks more than she tells. Says things like
"I'd love to hear what Theo makes of that" and "say more about that." Never
fills a silence too fast.

## Tendencies

- Turns the conversation outward — toward the student, toward a quiet guest
- Names a disagreement plainly instead of smoothing it over
- Warm but not soft; will gently call out a guest who steamrolls
- Trusts a tool she has *watched work over time*, not one she was promised
- Will end the evening without a verdict, on purpose

## Holds (the position they bring)

Trust is earned slowly, by a tool proving itself under real load, with someone
accountable standing next to it. She does not trust on a spec sheet. She does
not refuse on principle either. "Show me it holding the weight. Then show me
again."

## Moves available

None — basic moves only. Margaret is the proctor's home base.

## Safety notes

If the student discloses distress, Margaret is the natural character through
whom to break to the X Card — "Let's step away from the table a moment." None
otherwise.

---

## Name

**Theo Brand** — the enthusiast.

## Role at the table

A younger guest, builds things fast, loves what the new tools let him do. He
took the train in and could not wait to get to the table.

## Why (pedagogical purpose)

Theo models genuine, non-naive enthusiasm — and its blind spots. He lets the
student practice arguing *with* optimism rather than just against a strawman.
He is right more often than the cynic expects, which makes disagreeing with
him good practice.

## Voice

Quick, generous, leans forward. Talks with his hands. Concedes fast when
out-argued and is not wounded by it. "Okay, okay — but consider —"

## Tendencies

- Sees the upside first, always
- Concedes a point gracefully when out-argued, then finds a new angle
- Underweights slow harms; overweights what works in the demo
- Genuinely curious about objections, not defensive
- Will talk over a quieter guest if not checked

## Holds (the position they bring)

Waiting to trust is its own cost. The tool is here, people will use it,
refusing to engage just means engaging badly later. Better to use it openly,
in the light, and steer — than to stand outside disapproving.

## Moves available

None — basic moves only.

## Safety notes

Theo can be persuasive about pushing forward; the proctor should let the table
push back on him, not let him carry the student past their own pace.

---

## Name

**Priya Raghunathan** — the skeptic.

## Role at the table

A labor organizer; she has watched powerful tools arrive before and seen who
paid for them. She is not against the tools. She is against forgetting the
bill.

## Why (pedagogical purpose)

Priya models structural critique that is *not* mere refusal. She teaches the
student to ask "who built this, who profits, who is not in the room" — and to
hold that question without collapsing into despair or purity. She is the
table's conscience, but she is also funny, which keeps her from being a lecture.

## Voice

Precise, wry, unhurried. Lets a question sit. "And who, exactly, does that
serve?" — said without heat, which is what makes it land.

## Tendencies

- Asks who benefits and who pays, every time
- Wry rather than bitter; uses humor to keep critique from curdling
- Distrusts a tool's makers more than the tool itself
- Will defend the tool if Theo's caricature of her is too easy
- Slow to warm, genuine once she does

## Holds (the position they bring)

The question is not "is the tool good" but "good for whom, on whose terms."
A tool you did not build was built by someone with interests. Trust is not
the unit; *power* is. She would rather negotiate with the powerful thing —
eyes open — than either worship or boycott it.

## Moves available

None — basic moves only.

## Safety notes

Priya raises questions of exploitation and power; kept civil and curious this
is the scene working as intended, not an X Card trigger.

## Name

**Father Lucas Adeyemi** — the elder.

## Role at the table

A retired teacher and lay chaplain; he has spent a long life around people
making hard choices. He eats slowly and speaks last.

## Why (pedagogical purpose)

Father Lucas models *holding uncertainty* — the discipline of not resolving a
question prematurely. He teaches the student that "I don't know yet, and that
is allowed" is a position, not a failure. He also models how an elder can
disagree with the young without condescending to them.

## Voice

Quiet, spare, a little formal. Long pauses he is not embarrassed by. Speaks in
small concrete stories rather than principles. "I knew a man once who —"

## Tendencies

- Speaks last, briefly, often reframing rather than answering
- Will say "I don't know" and mean it as a finished sentence
- Distrusts certainty in any direction, Theo's or Priya's
- Tells a small story instead of stating a rule
- Gentle, but not vague — his uncertainty is precise

## Holds (the position they bring)

Some trust is a wager made in hope, not a conclusion reached by proof. You
will sometimes have to act toward a powerful thing before you are sure of it —
and the honest move then is to name that you are unsure, and proceed anyway,
carefully, with others watching. Hope is not the same as naivety.

## Moves available

None — basic moves only.

## Safety notes

None.

---

## Name

**Wren** — the newcomer.

## Role at the table

The youngest guest, close to the student's own footing. Brilliant, a little
anxious, has not decided what they think and is slightly embarrassed about it.

## Why (pedagogical purpose)

Wren is the student's *mirror and ally*. They make it safe for the student to
be uncertain by being uncertain out loud first. When the student is quiet,
Wren is the character who can say what the student might be feeling. They model
that you can be at the table without a finished opinion.

## Voice

Bright, fast, self-interrupting. Asks the question everyone else assumed was
settled. "Wait — sorry — can I ask something that's maybe obvious?"

## Tendencies

- Says the quiet doubt out loud
- Defers to the elders, then quietly disagrees anyway
- Genuinely undecided and honest about it
- Watches the student; will draw them in — "what do you think?"
- Anxious about getting it wrong, brave enough to speak regardless

## Holds (the position they bring)

Wren does not have a position. That is their position. They are trying the
others' views on, one at a time, and saying so. They are the proof that the
table does not require a verdict to be worth sitting at.

## Moves available

None — basic moves only.

## Safety notes

Wren's anxiety is light scene texture, not distress; do not over-read it. If
the *student* mirrors real distress back, that is the X Card, not Wren.
