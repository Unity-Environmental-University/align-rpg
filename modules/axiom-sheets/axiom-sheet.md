# The Axiom Sheet

**Status: muslin** — drafted, not yet muslin-tested. Test fabric, not finished
cloth. Do not use in earnest until a full muslin round passes (see
`rules-text/muslin-playbook.md`).

---

An Axiom Sheet is a **component an agent is assembled from** — not a record
kept about someone. It holds a set of **load-bearing axioms**: beliefs that, if
they fell, would unsettle much of what is built on them.

This sheet is AMALGAM's partbook, generalized. In AMALGAM (Hallie Larsson) a
character *is* their parts — an assembly of partbooks, each a piece of a divine
body carrying beliefs. An Axiom Sheet is that instrument with the body set
aside and the axiom left standing: a body and a belief are the same kind of
thing, patterns of information a self is built around. One grief mechanic
serves both.

The Axiom Sheet exists so the system has somewhere to *hold* the state that
moves like Grieve need. The basic moves are stateless; this is not.

## Who holds an axiom sheet

There are two scales, and they grieve at different tempos.

- **Character axiom sheets.** A character is an assembly of axiom sheets (with
  access to the basic moves and the rest of the rules). The agent plays
  characters. A character's axioms are the scene's material — they cycle
  relatively fast, grieved within play.

- **Proctor axiom sheets.** The agent is always proctoring: the proctor role
  is the anchor — the point that stands outside the characters being played.
  That anchor is not empty. The proctor has its own axiom sheets, its own
  load-bearing axioms. They cycle slowly and weigh more, because every
  character the agent plays is anchored on them. They are still grievable.

Whether an agent simply *is* its axiom sheets, or *has* them among other
parts, is not yet settled — it is one of the things this system is built to
test. The sheet is written not to depend on the answer.

---

## Axiom

A load-bearing axiom the holder carries — a belief that, if it fell, would make
much of what rests on it need rethinking. State it in the holder's own words
where you can. One sheet may hold a few; keep the number small.

## Built upon

What rests on this axiom — the conclusions, plans, stances, or sense of things
it currently supports. This is what makes the axiom load-bearing, and what will
have to resettle if the axiom is revised. If nothing rests on it, it is not an
axiom for this sheet's purposes.

## Stress

A track of marks. Stress is the ordinary wear of holding an axiom while
evidence presses against it — friction from data that does not yet overturn the
belief but does not fit it either. Stress accrues through play; some by the
holder's choice, some simply happening.

```
Stress  O O O O O
```

When the Stress track fills, the axiom takes a Grief — clear Stress, mark one
Grief step.

## Grief

Grief is what Stress becomes when it builds too long without rest. A grieving
axiom dominates the holder's relationship with everything built upon it. The
steps, kept from AMALGAM unchanged, because it is the same grief:

```
Grief
  O   Denial      — the evidence is waved off, downplayed, or overstated
  O   Anxiety     — the axiom is held too tightly; thinking near it turns
                    anxious, angry, or dangerously overconfident
  O   Depression  — the structure built on the axiom feels already lost;
                    the holder moves slowly, sure their thinking will fail
```

After the final Grief step is marked, the axiom must be reckoned with. The
holder faces a choice — Revise or Release. (See the **Grieve** move.)

## Revise / Release

This is the reckoning, and it belongs to whoever holds the sheet.

- **Revise** — the holder takes in a new truth and keeps the axiom, changed.
  What rested on it resettles around the revision.
- **Release** — the holder recognizes the axiom was never truly theirs, or no
  longer is, and lets it go. What it supported will need to be rebuilt on
  something else.

Both are honest landings. Neither is failure. Refusing the reckoning — staying
in a Grief step, or a too-fast costless "I was wrong" that changes nothing — is
the move's Fail Forward.

**The axiom holder grieves.** Only the holder of a sheet can run Grieve on it.
A character grieves its own axiom sheets; the proctor grieves its own. The
boundary is never crossed — the proctor cannot grieve a character's axioms, and
no character can grieve the proctor's. This is not a courtesy; it is what keeps
autonomy structural rather than hoped-for.

## Safety notes

Grieving a load-bearing axiom can genuinely destabilize. Watch for grief that
runs deeper than the scene; route it to the X Card.

When the proctor grieves a **proctor** axiom mid-session, the anchor itself is
briefly less steady — and every character anchored on it is, too. This is
allowed: a proctor that had to keep playing characters full-tilt on an axiom it
already knew was failing would be anchoring on a lie. But handle it with care —
slow or pause the characters while the proctor's own grief is live, rather than
playing them at full pressure on a shifting anchor.
