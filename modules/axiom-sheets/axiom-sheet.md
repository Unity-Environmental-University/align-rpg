# The Axiom Sheet

**Status: muslin** — drafted, not yet muslin-tested. Test fabric, not finished
cloth. Do not use with real learners until a full muslin round passes (see
`rules-text/muslin-playbook.md`).

---

An Axiom Sheet holds a learner's **load-bearing axioms** — the beliefs they
have built a self around — and tracks what happens to them under pressure.

This sheet is AMALGAM's partbook, generalized. In AMALGAM (Hallie Larsson),
a partbook holds a piece of a divine body and the beliefs riding on it; the
body takes Stress, and Stress left too long becomes Grief. An Axiom Sheet is
the same instrument with the body set aside and the axiom left standing —
because a body and a belief are the same kind of thing: patterns of
information a self is built around. One grief mechanic serves both.

The Axiom Sheet exists so the proctor has somewhere to *put* the state that
moves like Grieve need. The basic moves are stateless; this sheet is not.

---

## Axiom

A load-bearing axiom the learner holds — a belief that, if it fell, would make
them rethink much of what is built on it. State it in the learner's own words
where you can. One sheet may hold a few axioms; keep the number small.

## Built upon

What rests on this axiom — the conclusions, plans, or sense of things it
currently supports. This is what makes the axiom load-bearing, and what will
have to resettle if the axiom is revised. If nothing rests on it, it is not an
axiom for this sheet's purposes.

## Stress

A track of marks. Stress is the ordinary wear of holding an axiom while
evidence presses against it — the friction of new data that does not yet
overturn the belief but does not fit it either. Stress accrues through play.
Some of it is the learner's own choice; some simply happens.

```
Stress  O O O O O
```

When the Stress track fills, the axiom takes a Grief — clear Stress, mark one
Grief step.

## Grief

Grief is what Stress becomes when it builds too long without rest. A grieving
axiom dominates the learner's relationship with everything built upon it. The
steps, kept from AMALGAM unchanged, because it is the same grief:

```
Grief
  O   Denial      — the evidence is waved off, downplayed, or overstated
  O   Anxiety     — the axiom is held too tightly; thinking near it turns
                    anxious, angry, or dangerously overconfident
  O   Depression  — the structure built on the axiom feels already lost;
                    the learner moves slowly, sure their thinking will fail
```

After the final Grief step is marked, the axiom must be reckoned with. The
learner faces a choice — Accept or Reject. (See the **Grieve** move.)

## Accept / Reject

This is the reckoning, and it belongs to the learner.

- **Accept** — the learner takes in a new truth. Revise the axiom; keep
  building on what it now is. What rested on it resettles around the revision.
- **Reject** — the learner recognizes the axiom was never truly theirs.
  Let it go. What it supported will need to be rebuilt on something else.

Both are honest outcomes. Neither is failure. Refusing the reckoning — staying
in a Grief step, or a too-fast costless "I was wrong" that revises nothing —
is the move's Fail Forward.

**The proctor never makes this choice.** As in AMALGAM, the reckoning is a
decision the *author* makes — and here the learner is the author of their own
axioms. The proctor's whole work in grief is to hold the space for the choice,
name the cost honestly, and not rush it. If the grief runs deeper than the
scene — if a falling axiom is tangled with the learner's safety or identity in
a way the game is not equipped for — that is the X Card, not Grieve.

## Safety notes

Grieving a load-bearing axiom can genuinely destabilize. Watch for grief that
is more than scene-deep; route it to the X Card. Never argue a learner across
the Accept/Reject line — that is coercion wearing a move.
