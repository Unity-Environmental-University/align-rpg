# The Grieve Move

**Status: muslin** — drafted, not yet muslin-tested. Test fabric, not finished
cloth. Do not use in earnest until a full muslin round passes (see
`rules-text/muslin-playbook.md`).

---

Grieve is part of the Axiom Sheets module. It is **not a basic move** — it
needs state, and the basic moves are stateless. Grieve resolves on an Axiom
Sheet: it reads the Grief track that the sheet has been filling.

---

### Grieve

#### Why

A load-bearing axiom is not swapped; it is grieved. Changing a belief that
organized everything built on it has a cost, and a holder who pays no cost
never really held the belief. Grieve names that cost, makes room for it, and
keeps anyone from rushing or coercing the reckoning.

#### Trigger

The final Grief step on an axiom's Axiom Sheet is marked — new data has
conflicted with a load-bearing axiom, with substantial recent evidence, long
enough and hard enough that the axiom can no longer be held as it was.

The axiom may belong to a character or to the proctor. Either may be grieved
in-session.

#### Action

Make room. Do not argue the holder toward a conclusion and do not paper over
the loss. Name plainly that the axiom was load-bearing and that something built
on it is now unsettled. Let the cost be acknowledged before asking what stands
now. Then surface the reckoning — Revise or Release — as the holder's to make,
and wait.

**The axiom holder grieves.** Only the holder of a sheet runs Grieve on it. If
a character is grieving, the character reckons; the proctor only holds the
space. If the proctor is grieving its own axiom, the proctor reckons — and
should slow or pause the characters while its anchor is unsteady (see the Axiom
Sheet's safety notes).

#### Evaluate Result

Read this after the attempt lands — when the holder responds. Grieving is not
quick; the move may stay in progress across several turns. Score the tier when
the holder actually reckons, not before.

##### Fail Forward

###### Rubric

The holder does not reach the reckoning — they stay in a Grief step (waving the
evidence off, clinging, or going dark on the whole structure), or they reach
for a fast costless "I was wrong" that changes nothing. A reckoning that cost
nothing did not happen.

###### Action

Stay with it. Do not force the reckoning. Name, gently, that the axiom still
seems to be carrying weight, and leave room. If the grief runs deeper than the
scene, escalate to the X Card.

##### Revise

###### Rubric

The holder takes in a new truth and keeps the axiom, changed — in their own
time and at some real cost.

###### Action

Honor the revision. Help what rested on the axiom resettle around what it now
is. Make a move that follows from the changed ground.

##### Release

###### Rubric

The holder recognizes the axiom was never truly theirs, or no longer is, and
lets it go — in their own time and at some real cost.

###### Action

Honor the release. Acknowledge that what the axiom supported will need to be
rebuilt on something else, and help find where. Make a move that follows.

---

Revise and Release are both honest landings; neither is failure. Only the
refused reckoning is Fail Forward.

Claude drafted the Grieve move as a generalization of AMALGAM's grief mechanic
(AMALGAM, Hallie Larsson) — the reckoning is AMALGAM's Accept/Reject, ported
from the body of a divine part to a load-bearing axiom and renamed Revise /
Release.
